# Backlog of 2026-11-12

Ideas noted after the first release (`v0.1.0`). Nothing here is started; each item needs a short brief before work begins.

## Items

1. **Welcome screen.** _Done (2026-11-13)._ On the first visit, a screen explains what the app does and the two ways to start: an itinerary (points A to B) or a loop. It says the app is free and offers the donation (Liberapay link, `ABOUT_LINKS.donation`). It can be dismissed and found again (for example from the About window).
2. **Alternative loops on the map** _Done_ (briefed in [loop-duration.md](loop-duration.md)). The number of alternative loops is asked in the loop pop-up on the map, not only in the left panel.
3. **Loop by duration** _Done_ (briefed in [loop-duration.md](loop-duration.md)). A loop can be asked by time as well as by distance: a 1 hour run, a 3 hour ride. The duration is turned into a distance with the speed of the athlete, which depends on the profile (road, gravel, trail) and can be changed by the user. To decide: the default speeds, whether the speed takes the climb into account, and whether the duration is shown next to the distance on the result.
4. **Donation in the header.** _Done (2026-11-13)._ A donation button in the top bar, beside the About button, linking to the same Liberapay page. To decide: how discreet it is on a phone, where the header is already tight.

5. **Mobile drawer as a real sheet** _Done_ (briefed in [mobile-sheet.md](mobile-sheet.md), with item 8). On a phone the panel follows the finger: dragging it up or down moves it with the pointer, it settles on release with an animation (a swipe flick decides the direction), and it can cover the whole map when fully open. Today the swipe only toggles it.
6. **Elevation profile integrated on mobile.** _Done (light profile in the open sheet)._ Keep the fold-out profile over the map as today, and add a lighter version inside the open drawer: smaller, with fewer figures (distance, ascent, descent; no stretch selection or zoom buttons).
7. **Full screen on mobile.** _Done, then removed (2026-11-13): the button did not work on the author's Android phone and was judged useless._ A way to hide the chrome (header, buttons, help bar) to give the map the whole screen, for example for checking a route on the move. To study: the real uses (browsing the map, following a route) and whether the browser's own full screen API is needed or a hidden header is enough.
8. **Moving between map and panel.** _Done with item 5, except the bottom bar and the full-screen state (item 7)._ Proposals to compare in the brief, to make the change of mode as natural as possible on a phone:
   - three snap heights for the sheet (peek with the summary, half, full), as in common map apps, with the handle as the only control;
   - a tap on the route summary peeks the sheet up; a tap on the map lowers it to the peek;
   - the sheet goes to the peek by itself while the map is dragged or points are placed, and rises when a field is focused;
   - a bottom bar with two tabs (Map, Route) as a simpler alternative to the sheet;
   - the full-screen map mode (item 7) as a fourth state, left by a tap on a small button.

9. **Version in the About window.** _Done (2026-11-13)._ The About window shows the version of the app (for example "Version 0.1.0"). To decide: where it comes from (the `v*` tag the deploy builds from, injected at build time, rather than a number kept by hand) and what a local development build shows.

10. **Route with direction arrows.** _Done (2026-11-13), briefed in [route-arrows.md](route-arrows.md): chevrons on the selected route._ Arrows along the route line show which way it goes: out and back, or the way round a loop. Proposals to compare: small chevrons repeated every N pixels along the line (a MapLibre symbol layer with `symbol-placement: line`, hidden at low zoom); arrows only on the selected alternative; on an out-and-back, the way back drawn slightly offset or in a lighter tone so both directions can be told apart. To decide: density, colour over the surface layer, and whether the elevation cursor should also show the direction.
11. **Building a route point by point (exploration).** _Done (2026-11-13), briefed in [open-route.md](open-route.md)._ Today the route starts from a start and an end, then stops are inserted between them. The wish: go from Clermont-Ferrand to the Col du Béal, then on to Pavezin, without knowing the end first. To explore: an "extend the route" mode where each click or search adds a leg after the current last point, which becomes the end (what the stops do, but appended, with the end moving); a clear way to tell "add after the end" from "insert between" on the map (the ghost marker already says which role the next click has); how it works on a touch screen and with a loop; how undo and the elevation profile behave. Output of the exploration: a short brief with one recommended design.
12. **Descents too coloured on the profile.** _Done (2026-11-13), briefed in [profile-colours-selection.md](profile-colours-selection.md): descents neutral by default on road and gravel, coloured on trail, with a switch per profile._ The grade bands colour descents like climbs, which disturbs people who read the colours as effort. Proposals: (a) no colour on descents (neutral grey or the plain fill), colours for climbs only; (b) keep a descent colour but pale and in one hue (a light blue), without the steep levels; (c) a toggle "Colour the descents" in the profile header, off by default; (d) colour by effort (climbs by grade, flat neutral, descents neutral) and keep the grade figure in the stretch details. The recommendation is (a) with (c) as an option for those who want the descents back. The same choice applies to the colours of the route line if it ever uses grades.
13. **Resizable elevation profile.** On the map the profile becomes a drawer whose height the user changes by dragging its top edge (with keys on the handle), remembered on the device. Its content follows the height: very low, only the thin curve and the cursor figure; medium, the curve with the axes and the grade bands; tall, add the surface bar, the stretch details and the zoom buttons. To decide: minimum and maximum heights (a share of the screen, as the 45 dvh cap today), how it combines with the mobile sheet (items 5 and 6) and a snap to three heights as for the sheet.
14. **Draw-the-loop button text off-centre (fix)** _Done (2026-11-13)._ In the loop dialog, the text of "Draw the loop" is not centred in its button. Small CSS fix: check `.loop-choice` (text-align, the `strong` inside, flex centring) in both the dialog and the panel.

15. **Installable app (PWA).** The app can be installed on a phone or a desktop, from its own button as well as from the browser's menu. A manifest (`apps/web/public/manifest.webmanifest`) and a service worker already exist, so the work is to make installability complete and checked: run a Lighthouse installability audit on the production site; add what stores and browsers ask for beyond the basics (`id`, `screenshots` for the richer install dialog on Android and desktop Chrome, `categories`, `shortcuts` such as "Make a loop", `lang` and a French name through `name` localisation or a second manifest); iOS tags (`apple-touch-icon`, `apple-mobile-web-app-capable`, status bar style) since Safari ignores part of the manifest; an "Install the app" button that uses the `beforeinstallprompt` event where it exists (Chrome, Edge, Android) and, on iPhone, a short hint "Share, then Add to Home Screen"; where it lives (About window, welcome screen). To decide: whether the button appears in the header or only in About, and the theme colour in standalone mode (the header green).

16. **Lighter header on a phone.** _Done with proposal B (logo over the map, a menu with the offline actions)._ Today a phone shows two bands before the map: the header (logo, name, API dot, language select, heart, "i") and the offline bar (two buttons on two lines), about 6.5 rem in all. Proposals to compare:
    - **A. One slim bar (recommended).** About 2.75 rem: the logo alone (no name), the API dot folded into the logo as a small coloured badge, one round "menu" button on the right. The menu (a popover or a sheet) holds: language (two or three plain choices instead of a select), support (the heart), About, and the offline actions. The offline bar disappears from the page.
    - **B. Logo as an overlay on the map.** No header at all: the logo is a small round button floating top left over the map (like undo and redo), opening the same menu. The whole screen goes to the map, a step before the full-screen mode; the cost is that the menu button competes with undo, redo and full screen for the top corner.
    - **C. Keep the header, thin it.** Logo and a short name, language as a two-letter button (FR/EN) that flips, heart and "i" kept, the offline buttons moved into the sheet (Saved tab) where saving a route already lives. The least change, saves about 3 rem.
    - **Saving routes and the view offline**: whichever option, one entry point, "Keep offline", with two choices inside (this route, this view) in the menu or in the Saved tab, with the same status text as today.
      To decide: A or B, where the offline actions go, and whether the language needs a menu at all (a two-letter flip button is enough for two languages).
